Russian interference and political meme pages on Twitter

A entry in coordinated online influence: impersonation, divisive messaging, and the limits of claims about impact.

← Back to United States

About the meme

During the 2016 US election, Twitter accounts later linked by investigations to Russia’s Internet Research Agency (IRA) posted political messages using different names and identities. Their posts included memes, images, hashtags, links, and replies aimed at audiences with different political views.

Investigations described a wider social-media operation, not one single style of meme. Some accounts supported or attacked political figures, criticised news organisations, or tried to look like ordinary American users. Some posts amplified existing divisions. This does not mean every controversial meme or every pro-Trump post came from Russia.

One item in this archive is a graphic featuring Jesus and Hillary Clinton that the metadata connects to the Russian-linked “Army of Jesus” page. It combines religious imagery with a direct attack on Clinton. The connection applies to this recorded item and should not be extended to unrelated memes without evidence.
