Pepe the Frog: origin, appropriation, weaponisation, and afterlife

From a comic character and reaction image to a contested political symbol—and why no single interpretation fits every Pepe meme.

← Back to United States

About the meme

Artist Matt Furie created Pepe the Frog for his comic Boy’s Club, which began in the mid-2000s. The laid-back frog became popular online through the expression “Feels good man.” People cropped and reposted Pepe panels, changing the captions and expressions to show happiness, sadness, smugness, frustration, and many other feelings.

By the mid-2010s, some far-right and alt-right online communities had made versions of Pepe with racist, antisemitic, Nazi, and other hateful imagery. During the 2016 US election, Pepe appeared in pro-Trump memes. In September 2016, the Anti-Defamation League added Pepe to its hate-symbol database, while noting that the character was not inherently hateful and that context mattered.

Furie objected to racist uses of the character and took part in the #SavePepe campaign. In 2017, he published a comic strip showing Pepe in a coffin, as a symbolic farewell to the hateful version of the character. Pepe continued to circulate, however, and has also been used in unrelated political settings, including pro-democracy demonstrations in Hong Kong.
