About me

My name is Harshavardhan Pachani, and I am pursuing an M.A. in Liberal Arts at Indian Institute of Technology Guwahati. I created this website as part of my Master's project, which documents political memes used during elections.

The project is important because political memes are cultural and political artefacts that can communicate ideas, shape political framing, and circulate through digital spaces. By bringing together meme images, contextual explanations, source links, and structured metadata, this archive helps preserve and interpret these materials for further research.

Purpose and scope

This website documents political memes as cultural and political artefacts. It combines images, contextual explanations, source links, and a structured metadata record to support further research.

The metadata spreadsheet is used to identify and describe items: dates, platforms, source URLs, transcribed text, rhetorical frames, and contextual notes. These fields support the entries but are not intended to replace them.

How to interpret a meme

Ask what is visible, what the caption says, who is represented, where the item appeared, and what can be independently verified. Distinguish the meme’s framing from factual claims about the world. Circulation does not automatically establish persuasion or impact.

Limitations

The current collection is a small, evolving sample. Dates may represent when an item was observed rather than when it was first created. Some platforms or source URLs may change or become unavailable. Claims about authorship, coordinated activity, reach, and audience effects require additional evidence beyond a single image or metadata row.

Where relevant, the archive uses the term “mimetic weaponisation” for purposeful use of memes to signal belonging, attack opponents, or normalise exclusionary political framings. This is an analytical lens, not a label automatically applied to every meme.
